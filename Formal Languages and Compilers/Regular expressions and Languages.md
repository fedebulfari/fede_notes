# Regular Expressions and Languages

- **Regular languages**: simplest family of formal languages.
- **Regular expression (regexp)**: string over $\Sigma$ plus meta-symbols, used to *generate* a language.
- A language is **regular** iff it is generated by some regexp.

## 1. Syntax of Regexps

A regexp $r$ over terminal alphabet $\Sigma$ is defined by:

1. $r = \emptyset$
2. $r = a$ with $a \in \Sigma$ (also $\epsilon$ is allowed, since $\emptyset^* = \{\epsilon\}$)
3. $r = (s \cup t)$ — union, often written $s \mid t$
4. $r = (s \cdot t)$ — concatenation, often written $st$
5. $r = (s)^*$ — star

where $s, t$ are regexps.

> [!tip] Operator precedence
> 1. star $^*$
> 2. concatenation $\cdot$
> 3. union $\cup$ / $\mid$
>
> So $ab^* \cup c$ means $((a \cdot (b^*)) \cup c)$.

## 2. Meaning of a Regexp

The meaning $L(r)$ is a language over $\Sigma$:

| regexp $r$     | language $L(r)$  |
| -------------- | ---------------- |
| $\emptyset$    | $\emptyset$      |
| $\epsilon$     | $\{\epsilon\}$   |
| $a \in \Sigma$ | $\{a\}$          |
| $s \cup t$     | $L(s) \cup L(t)$ |
| $s.t$          | $L(s).L(t)$      |
| $s^*$          | $L(s)^*$         |

> [!example] Multiples of three
> - $e = (111)^*$ generates $L_{e}=\{\epsilon, 111, 111111, \dots\} = \{1^n \mid n \bmod 3 = 0\}$
> - $e = 11(1)^*$ generates strings with $n \ge 2$

> [!example] Signed integers
> Let $\Sigma = \{+,-,d\}$ where $d$ is a decimal digit. Then $e = (+\cup -\cup \epsilon)dd^*$ generates $L(e) = \{+,-,\epsilon\}\{d\}\{d\}^*$.

## 3. REG vs FIN

- **REG**: family of all regular languages.
- **FIN**: family of all finite-cardinality languages.

> [!warning] Caution
> REG and FIN are **sets of languages** (sets of sets of strings), not sets of strings.

> [!info]
> Every finite language is regular: finite union of strings, each string a finite concatenation of symbols.
> REG also contains infinitely many infinite languages.
> Hence $\mathrm{FIN} \subset \mathrm{REG}$.

## 4. Property of Regexp

- **Subexpression**: fully parenthesize $r$, number symbols, then isolate subexpressions.
- **Choice**: union and iteration have alternatives; iteration offers $0,1,2,\dots$ repetitions. Choosing one gives a smaller language.
- **Derivation** $e' \Rightarrow e''$: replace a subexpression $\beta$ in $e' = \alpha\beta\gamma$ by a choice $\delta$ of $\beta$, giving $e'' = \alpha\delta\gamma$. 
	Steps chain: $e_0 \Rightarrow e_1 \Rightarrow \dots \Rightarrow e_n$.

> [!info]  
> $e_{0}\implies^+e_{n}, \ e_{0}$ derive $e_n$ in $n\geq1$ steps	
> $e_{0}\implies^*e_{n},\ e_{0}$ derive $e_n$ in $n\geq 0$ steps

- The **language generated** by a regexp is: $L(r) = \{ x \in \Sigma^* \mid r \Rightarrow^* x \}$ 
	only derivations ending in pure terminal strings count.
- **Equivalence**: Two regexps are equivalent iff they generate the same language. A derived regexp defines a subset of the original.
- **Ambiguity**: a phrase obtainable by two substantially different choice sequences. Formal check via marked regexp $f^\##$: if two marked strings project to the same unmarked string, $f$ is ambiguous.

## 5. Other Operators

Basic: union, concatenation, star. 
Derived:
- **Power**: $e^h = \underbrace{e \dots e}_{h \text{ times}}$
- **Cross**: $e^+ = ee^*$
- **Repetition**: rom $k$ to $n$ times — $[e]_{k}^n=a^k\cup\dots\cup a^n$: f
- **Optionality**: $[e] = \epsilon \cup e$
- **Interval**: e.g. `(0…9)`, `(a…z)`

**Extended regexps** allow $\cap$, $\lnot$ (complement), $-$ (difference). They add no expressive power over REG but are more concise.

> [!example] Intersection as constraint
> Alphabet $\{a,b\}$: strings containing $bb$ AND of even length:
> $$(a \cup b)^* bb (a \cup b)^* \cap ((a \cup b)(a \cup b))^*$$

> [!example] Complement via De Morgan
> $r = (a \cup b)^*$ minus strings starting with $b$, ending with $a$, or containing $aa$ or $bb$.

## 6. Closure of REG

> [!info] Closed family
> A family $\mathcal{F}$ is **closed** w.r.t. operator $\theta$ iff applying $\theta$ to languages in $\mathcal{F}$ stays in $\mathcal{F}$.

- REG is closed under concatenation, union, star — hence also cross, power, optionality.
- REG is the **smallest** family containing all finite languages and closed under concatenation, union, star.
- REG is also closed under intersection, complement, difference, mirroring (easier via automata).
- Strict containment: $\mathrm{REG} \subset \mathrm{LIB}$ (context-free languages).

## 7. Linguistic Abstraction

Replace effective alphabet (keywords, identifiers, …) giving them a simpler form called **abstract representation**.
Compiler design works on the abstract language.
Most artificial-language structures are compositions of few paradigms via concatenation, union, iteration.

### 7.1. Lists

A list is a sequence of unbounded length: $e^+$ (or $e^*$ if empty list allowed). Element $e$ may be atomic or itself a list.
- With separators / start / end markers, e.g.:
	- `begin istr (; istr)* end`
	- `STAMPA(par (, par)*)`
	- `[int (, int)*]`

### 7.2. Substitution

Replaces each terminal $b$ of a source language with phrases of a target language $L_b$ over $\Delta$, producing a language over $(\Sigma \setminus \{b\}) \cup \Delta$.

### 7.3. Nested Lists

Lists can contain lower-level lists, e.g. level 1 `begin istr ; istr end`, level 2 `STAMPA(var, var, …, var)`.
