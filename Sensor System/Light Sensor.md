# 1. Photodiode

Device with two terminals that produce a current when solicited by photons
![[Light Sensor-1791980180271.webp|455x267]]

Is sed with a reversed bias(voltage at cathode>voltage at anode) this in order to better distinguish the different current generated
![[Light Sensor-1791980682491.webp|455]]

## Current Generation

![[Light Sensor-1791980956918.webp]]

Having the reversed bias the  electrons travel to the cathode, therefore they will have low probability to recombine.

## P-N vs P-I-N

![[Light Sensor-1791981591420.webp|555]]

p-i-n have an intrinsic layer between p and n , this means that the depletion region will be thicker.
This means higher sensibility and reduced capacitance(that usually introduce problem with instability of the circuite).

## Penetration Depth

**Penetration depth**: depth at which the intensity of the radiation inside the material falls to $\frac{1}{e}$ (about 37%) of its original value at the surface.
![[Light Sensor-1791981848603.webp|383]]

## Responsivity

![[Light Sensor-1791984039609.webp|389]]
**Photodiode Responsivity (sensitivity)**: ratio of the photocurrent $I_{ph}$ to the incident light power $P$ at a given wavelength: $$R_{\lambda}=\frac{I_{ph}}{P}$$
$R_{\lambda}$ depends on incident light wavelength, $V_{rev}$ and Temperature.
![[Light Sensor-1791984216910.webp|302]]
